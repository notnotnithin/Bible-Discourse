### Challenges in Interpreting Revelation

- **The source of confusion and intimidation**
    - Much of the difficulty surrounding the book stems from "imposed interpretations"
    - These are interpretations not actually based on the text itself
- **Impact of imposed interpretations**
    - They can rob readers of the inspiration, encouragement, and confidence needed to engage with the book
    - This often leads people to avoid the book entirely or approach it with fear

### Addressing Mainstream Interpretations

- **The nature of popular interpretations**
    - Many current interpretations are highly popular and considered "mainstream" within both Protestant and Catholic theology
    - These are often treated as the "gold standard" of Revelation scholarship
- **The need for a critical approach**
    - Despite their popularity, some of these interpretations are baseless and lack biblical roots
    - This session aims to address these "bad teachings" because they are invalid, not to engage in unproductive or cranky nitpicking
    - The goal is to correct the course of interpretation rather than simply delegitimizing others

### Drivers of Poor Theology

- **The influence of personal experience**
    - Many people form their theology based on their own experiences
    - **[Why this is a risk]** Because people tend to embrace or reject beliefs based on how they make them feel (good or bad)
- **Primary drivers of bad teaching**
    - Much of the poor theology in the body of Christ is driven by two things:
        - Pain
        - Money
- **Tiers of authority**
    - The Bible is the highest authority
    - The Bible interprets the Bible; nothing else does

### The Hierarchy of Interpretation

- **The proper order of authority**
    - 1. Scripture (The highest authority)
    - 2. Church History (Relevant and informative, but not binding)
    - 3. Personal subjective feelings (The least authoritative)
- **The role of church history**
    - It provides insight into what the church has believed and debated over the last 2,000 years
    - It offers context on long-standing issues such as:
        - The divinity of Jesus
        - The nature of the cross
        - The resurrection
        - The role of women in the church
- **The modern inversion of authority**
    - Modernity often flips the hierarchy on its head
    - **[Why this is a problem]** Instead of starting with the Bible, people often start with "How does this make me feel?" and then seek validation from others or history, rather than letting the text lead.
- **Guidance for studying Revelation**
    - To engage with this study effectively, one must intentionally:
        - Lay down personal feelings
        - Set aside emotional or intellectual reactions
        - Allow the Bible to be the authoritative voice

### The Dual Nature of Church History

- **[The Blessing]** Church history is valuable because throughout the centuries, many people have studied the Word of God deeply, providing rich historical interpretation.
- **[The Curse]** The church has historically been "out to lunch" on certain regular issues, such as biblical eschatology, meaning it is not authoritative in an ultimate sense.
- **The marginalization of Revelation**
    - Even during the Reformation—often viewed as the pinnacle of theological thought—many key leaders did not believe the Book of Revelation should even be considered part of Scripture or worthy of interpretation.
    - This historical neglect has trickled down through the centuries, contributing to the modern challenges in interpreting the book.

    - **[The basis of His authority]** He has conquered death: "I died and behold I am alive forevermore. I have the keys to death and hell"
- **The scope of the revelation**
    - The text establishes a transition from what has been seen to what is coming:
        - "...the things that you've seen"
        - "...those that are"
        - "...those that are to take place after this"

### A Relational and Discipleship-Oriented Approach

- **[The Centrality of Jesus]** The book is titled "the revelation of Jesus Christ and the things that must take place"
    - The study focuses on both the Man (Jesus) and the events
    - **[Why this matters]** If you do not see Jesus clearly, you cannot understand the events because He is the center of all of them
    - Jesus serves as the ultimate interpretive guide, shepherd, master, king, and Lord
- **The goal of study**
    - The objective is not merely to be intellectually correct or to possess "good eschatology"
    - The goal is to approach the book from a relational perspective and through the lens of discipleship
    - **[The ultimate aim]** To follow the footsteps of the crucified and risen Messiah and walk out His commands

### Interpreting the Symbols of Revelation

- **[The visual nature of the text]** The Book of Revelation is fundamentally a visual book, characterized by the things seen in visions
    - The text explicitly references these visual elements: "the things that you've seen"
- **The first interpretive challenge**
    - The study begins by addressing a core question: How do you interpret the symbols used throughout the book?
    - **[The first example]** The mystery of the seven stars held in the right hand of Jesus
        - "As for the mystery of the seven stars that you saw in my right hand..."

### The Simplicity of Interpreting Symbolism

- **[The interpretive challenge]** While the mystery surrounding the book's imagery can seem daunting, the speaker argues that interpretation is actually very simple
    - **[Reason 1: Biblical Continuity]** The imagery is not new; it is pulled from the Old Testament, which already contains its own established interpretations
    - **[Reason 2: Internal Keys]** The text provides its own interpretive keys, specifically in Revelation 1:20
- **The key in Revelation 1:20**
    - This verse acts as a decoder for the rest of the book's symbolism
    - **[Direct Interpretations]**
        - The seven stars = the angels of the seven churches
        - The seven lampstands = the seven churches

### The Self-Interpreting Nature of Revelation

- **[The core principle]** Interpretation is simplified because the text provides its own answers
    - Every time a symbol is presented to John, the Lord or an angel provides a direct explanation
    - **[Example]** After seeing the seven stars and seven lampstands, the text clarifies their meaning immediately
- **[A guarantee for the student]** You will not find symbols in Revelation that lack a corresponding interpretation within the book itself

### Introduction to Hermeneutics

- **Hermeneutics** is the method or way used to measure and interpret Scripture

### Distinguishing Symbols from Literal Descriptions

- **[A shift in perspective]** Rather than trying to impose external meanings onto the text, one should allow Revelation to interpret itself
- **[Challenging the 'symbolism' assumption]** Contrary to popular belief, the book is actually relatively low on symbols
    - **[The danger of over-symbolizing]** A common mistake is treating literal descriptions as metaphors
        - **[Example: The Trumpets]** When the trumpets cause water to turn to blood, it is not a metaphor for something else
        - **[Literal reality]** It means actual water becomes actual blood, much like the events during the Exodus
- **The interpretive question**
    - Since many descriptions are literal, how does one distinguish between a true symbol and a literal description within the text?

### The Literal Nature of Judgment Events

- **[Literal vs. Metaphorical]** The judgment events described in Revelation (seals, trumpets, and bowls) are literal occurrences rather than purely symbolic metaphors
    - While some might interpret them as purely metaphorical, the speaker argues they mean exactly what they say
- **The use of analogies**
    - Not every description is a literal physical object; some are analogies used to describe the *nature* of an event
    - **[Example: The Sky as a Scroll]** When the text says the sky rolls up like a scroll:
        - It does not mean the sky becomes a physical cylinder or paper tube
        - It means the sky is being physically contorted or changed by end-time events

### A Better Interpretive Question

- **[Moving beyond the binary]** Rather than asking if a description is literal or symbolic, the more important question is: "What does it mean?"
    - The goal is to understand the intended meaning and the nature of the thing being described.
- **[Example: The sound of Jesus' voice]** The text describes His voice as being "like the sound of many waters"
    - A strictly literal interpretation (that He literally makes river noises with His mouth) misses the point
    - The description is meant to convey the power, scale, or quality of the sound
- **[Key to unlocking symbols]** Verse 20 serves as a "permutable key" for interpreting symbols like the seven stars, as it provides the necessary context to understand their meaning within the text.

### Contextual Interpretation of Symbols

- **[The primary method]** Interpretation should not rely on external searches or outside speculation
    - The core teaching is that the text of Revelation interprets itself
    - The surrounding context within the scripture provides the necessary answers
- **[Example: The seven stars]** To understand what the seven stars represent, one simply looks at the immediate verse context
    - The text explicitly states that the seven stars are seven angels

### Major and Minor Symbols

- **[Symbolic hierarchy]** Not all symbols in the book carry the same weight in the narrative
    - There are a few major symbols that drive the story forward
    - There are also various minor symbols that are present but do not play a primary role in the unfolding of the plot
- **[Examples of minor symbols]**
    - The seven stars
    - The seven lampstands

### The Nature of the Scroll Symbolism

- **[Minor vs. Major Symbolism]** The act of John eating the scroll is considered a minor symbol
    - It does not necessarily mean he is eating a physical, literal scroll
    - Instead, it represents an encounter in a realm between reality and non-reality, similar to the experiences of Daniel or Abraham
- **[The purpose of the symbol]** The focus should not be on the literalness of the scroll, but on the experience of "consuming" the message
    - When John imbibes the content, it makes his stomach feel both bitter and sweet
    - **[The Bitter and the Sweet]** This reflects the dual nature of the Book of Revelation: it contains both awful and great things that must be both consumed and proclaimed.

### The Visual Nature of Revelation

- **[Inherent accessibility]** The book's imagery is surprisingly easy to understand
    - Even children can grasp the core meaning of the symbols
    - **[The adult obstacle]** Adults often struggle because they attempt to process the text through a humanistic and naturalistic intellectual lens rather than accepting the visual narrative
- **[A mental model for reading]** To engage with the content effectively, one can imagine the book as a film being projected on a screen
    - This aligns with the biblical instruction to "write the things that you see"
    - Visual communication serves as a powerful mode for conveying the book's message

### Avoiding Interpretive Over-Complication

- **[Keep it simple]** To avoid getting lost in rigid debates over whether something is literal or symbolic, one should approach the text with simplicity and clarity
    - A helpful mental exercise is to involve a child's perspective to maintain focus on the core message
    - **[The risk of intellect]** Over-analyzing through a strictly humanistic or intellectual lens can cause the actual meaning to be lost

### The Seven Major Symbols

- **[Structural importance]** There are seven major symbols that are considered "structural" because they play a dynamic role in the unfolding of the story
- **[Self-interpretation]** A key feature of these major symbols is that they are interpreted by the scriptures themselves
    - This means the reader does not necessarily need to consult outside sources or search engines to understand them
    - The text provides its own context and definitions
- **[Example: The Dragon]**
    - Introduced in Revelation chapter 12
    - Acts as a key character within the narrative

### Major Symbols in Revelation

- **[The Dragon]** A visually dominant figure appearing primarily between chapters 12 and 20
    - Unlike some symbols that require speculation, the text explicitly defines the dragon
    - **[Identity]** The dragon is identified as "the serpent of all the name is Satan"
- **[The Woman with a Male Child]** Another major symbol introduced in Revelation chapter 12
    - This symbol draws on Old Testament imagery, specifically referencing the family of Abraham and Sarah
    - **[Connection to Israel]** The imagery aligns with the 12 stars seen in Genesis, which represent the 12 tribes of Israel

### Self-Interpretation of Revelation 12 Symbols

- **[Connecting to Genesis]** The symbols in Revelation 12 utilize the same imagery and descriptions found in the book of Genesis
    - In Genesis, the vision of the woman is interpreted as the 12 tribes of Israel
    - Therefore, in Revelation 12, the woman represents national, ethnic, territorial, and tribal Israel (the covenant people)
- **[The Identity of the Combatants]**
    - **The Dragon**: Identified as Satan
    - **The Woman**: Identified as Israel
- **[The Male Child and Psalm 2]**
    - The male child born of the woman is described as ruling the nations with a rod of iron and dashing them to pieces like a potter's vessel
    - This is a direct quotation/reference to **Psalm 2**
    - **[Psalm 2 Context]** The text describes the nations raging against the Lord and His Anointed One (the Messiah), who responds by dashing the nations like a potter's vessel

### The Beast (The Antichrist)

- Introduced in Revelation chapter 13
- **[Identity]** The Beast is interpreted as the Antichrist
    - This is one of the most dominant figures in prophetic scripture
    - While many "antichrists" have appeared throughout history (as noted in 1 John 2), the Beast refers to a specific, future, singular figure
- **[Prophetic Descriptions]** The character is defined by a collection of imagery drawn from Ezekiel, Daniel, and 2 Thessalonians
    - Often referred to by various titles, including:
        - The tyrant
        - The one who sets up the abomination of desolation
        - The final little horn of the man of sin
    - Ultimately defined as the final aggressor against the people of God

### The False Prophet (The Second Beast)

- Identified as the "other beast" in Revelation 13
- **[Relationship to the Antichrist]** The text describes a specific relationship between the first beast and this second beast, detailing their joint functions on the world stage
- **[Role]** They serve as the two final antagonists against:
    - The people of Israel
    - The people of the covenant
    - The nations of the earth

### Harlot Babylon

- Known by various names depending on the translation, such as "Mystery Babylon" or "The Great Prostitute"
- **[Definition]** A global, demonized religious and financial network
- **[Relationship to the Antichrist]** The Antichrist has a unique relationship with the Harlot
    - During the beginning of the final tribulation of this age, the Antichrist dies and subsequently burns her with fire

### Characteristics of Harlot Babylon

- Defined as an international network characterized by several key traits:
    - **Violence**: It is responsible for shedding the blood of the saints
    - **Wealth**: Its great wealth causes the nations of the earth to marvel at her
    - **Immorality**: It is marked by incredible sexual immorality
- **[The Triple Threat]** The entity is a complex intersection of three components:
    - Religious iniquity
    - Moral/sexual immorality
    - Financial influence that draws in the nations
- **[Complexity]** Revelation 17 and 18 are noted as some of the most complicated and mysterious chapters in the book due to the intense level of detail provided regarding the final harlot

### The Seven Heads

- The sixth major symbol introduced in the text
- The identity and nature of these heads are defined in the subsequent verses

### The Seven Heads and Ten Horns

- **[The Seven Heads]** Represent seven regional, territorial empires that have persecuted Israel throughout history
    - These empires exist in a chronological succession
    - The pattern involves one empire being replaced by another
    - This cycle is described in Revelation 17 as the "falling and rising" of the final beast empire
- **[The Ten Horns]** Represent ten nations
    - These nations come into alignment with the final Antichrist (the Beast)
    - Their purpose is to attack the Harlot at the end of the age
    - This alignment is supported by references in Ezekiel 38 and Psalm 83

### Navigating the Symbolism of Revelation

- The abundance of symbolism should not be intimidating
    - There are only seven major symbols
    - All minor symbols are also interpreted by Scripture itself
    - These symbols do not alter the core structure of the story
- **[Caution]** Avoid explaining away the core meaning of central figures
    - Altering the fundamental identity of figures like the Antichrist, the Harlot, or Israel changes the entire message
    - The goal is to let the text speak for itself to maintain clarity

### False Teachings and the Message of Revelation

- Certain false teachings act like "beer goggles," distorting how one perceives the book's symbolism
- These teachings prevent an intellectual and accurate interaction with the message of Revelation

### The Impact of False Teachings on Interpretation

- Certain theological frameworks act as "beer goggles," distorting the perception of Revelation's symbolism
- **[The Pre-Tribulation Rapture]** This teaching is identified as a primary factor eroding the ability to interact with the book as intended
    - It was popularized in the 1800s and furthered by a famous study Bible in the 1900s
    - While it has lost luster globally, it remains strong in certain pockets of American evangelicalism
- **[Consequences of Distortion]** If the belief is held that the church will be removed before the tribulation, it fundamentally misrepresents the text:
    - It distorts the understanding of who the Antichrist is
    - It misrepresents what the church should do in response to these realities
    - It prevents an accurate emotional and practical interaction with the symbols and the generation of the Lord's return

### The Toxicity of the Rapture Doctrine

- The pre-tribulation rapture remains a significant teaching within American evangelicalism, despite its lack of biblical basis
- **[Impact on the Church]** This teaching is described as toxic to the Body of Christ
    - It is not taught in the Bible; there are no specific verses that support the removal of the church before the tribulation
    - It often generates vitriol and intense negative reactions when its spiritual integrity is questioned

### The Persecuted Church's View of the Rapture

- The persecuted church generally rejects the pre-tribulation rapture doctrine
    - It is viewed as a teaching cherished by those in comfortable Western contexts
    - It offers a false sense of security, proclaiming that believers will be removed before things get difficult
- **[The Logical Fallacy]** The doctrine relies on a misapplication of 1 Thessalonians:
    - The Bible states believers are not appointed to wrath
    - The pre-tribulation teaching imposes this by arguing that because the tribulation is characterized by wrath, believers must be removed before it starts
    - This treats the wrath of God as a mechanical event, whereas the speaker notes that God's wrath has been poured out in every generation since the beginning of time

### The Distinction Between Wrath and Tribulation

- The wrath of God is not merely a future eschatological event but a historical continuum that will climax at the end of the age
- **[The Saints' Position]** While believers will be present on earth during the outpouring of God's wrath (witnessing the seals, trumpets, and bowls), they are not appointed to drink the cup of that wrath
    - This distinction was secured by Jesus on the cross
- **[The Reality of Tribulation]** Although not appointed to wrath, believers *are* appointed to suffer tribulation
    - The speaker cites several scriptural foundations for this reality:
        - Acts 14: "By tribulation do we inherit the kingdom of God"
        - John 16:1: "In this world you will have tribulation, but take heart; I have overcome the world"
        - 2 Timothy 3:4: References great tribulation in the latter days
        - Revelation 1:9: John identifies himself as being "in tribulation"
        - Romans 8:35-37: Believers are more than conquerors through tribulation

### Preterism

- A theological teaching that claims the Book of Revelation was fulfilled in the past
    - The term is derived from the linguistic concept of a "preterite" verb, which refers to the past tense
- **[The Core Claim]** Preterists argue that the events described in Revelation have already occurred in history
    - For example, they may identify the Beast as a historical figure, such as the Roman Emperor Nero
- **[The Counter-Argument]** The speaker notes a significant discrepancy between historical accounts and biblical prophecy:
        - While some historical figures died in ways that do not align with scripture (e.g., Nero committing suicide), the Bible describes the Antichrist being killed by the "breath of Jesus' mouth" upon His return to establish His thrones

### Critiques of Literalism and the Preterist Argument

- **[The 'Barbaric' Critique]** Critics often reject the literal return of Jesus to defeat the Antichrist, labeling the concept as "barbaric."
- **[The Preterist Use of History]** A major argument for the preterist view relies on the destruction of the Temple in 70 AD to explain the prophecies in Revelation 11
    - Proponents attempt to map specific historical events to the seals, trumpets, and other symbols using historical records
- **[The Timeline Discrepancy]** The speaker notes a tension between the 70 AD event and the text of Revelation 11:
    - Revelation 11 describes a period of 42 months of destruction that occurs *after* the temple is destroyed
    - This period leads to the ultimate transition where "the kingdoms of this world, become the kingdoms of our God and his Christ, and he wills over everything forever"

### Preterism and Metaphorical Reinterpretation

- **[The Interpretive Method]** Preterism is predicated on the idea that biblical descriptions are often metaphorical rather than literal
    - Proponents argue that many prophecies were fulfilled within the first generation of believers
- **[Example: The Coming of Christ]** The speaker cites a popular argument (referencing a British scholar) regarding the description of Jesus coming on the clouds in Daniel, Mark, Matthew, and Luke:
    - **Literal interpretation:** Jesus physically descends from the sky.
    - **Preterist interpretation:** The phrase is a metaphor meaning Jesus was actually *going away* on the clouds (ascending) rather than returning to earth.

### Amillennialism

- A theological view that denies the existence of the millennium described in scripture
    - The speaker compares the term to "atheism" (denying God) by suggesting it denies the reality of the millennium
- **[The Biblical Millennium]** Based on Revelation 20, it is a thousand-year period characterized by:
    - Jesus establishing His rule on earth
    - The restoration of all things
    - This period occurs before the final judgment and the ultimate, final restoration of all things

### The Progressive Nature of the Millennium

- A millennial teaching is supported by the collective testimony of the prophets, not just Revelation.
    - The prophets spoke of the Messiah coming to progressively restore all things through His throne in Jerusalem.
- **[The Process of Restoration]** The return of Jesus is described as a process rather than an instantaneous transformation:
    - Jesus will not simply "snap his fingers" to make everything wonderful
    - Instead, there will be "very human things" and dynamics that take place as He restores the created order
- **[Biblical Examples of Post-Return Activities]**
    - **Psalm 110**: Jesus will execute heads of state
    - **Matthew 25**: Jesus will gather and judge the nations
    - **Ezekiel 38 and 39**: Post-return activities include cleaning up remains (for seven months), anointing/cleaning the temple, and the rebuilding of Jerusalem
    - **Rebuilding Jerusalem**: People from North Africa, the Middle East, and the Arabian Peninsula will bring goods to rebuild the city from the rubble of the final tribulation

### The Impact of Amillennialism on Scripture

- Denies the literal substance of millennial prophecies
    - Claims there is no physical thousand-year reign on earth
    - Rejects the idea of Jesus reigning on a physical throne to hold nations accountable
- **[The Spiritual Reinterpretation]** Amillennialism posits that the millennium is happening "now" in a way that is:
    - Spiritual
    - Metaphorical
    - Nebulous and ambiguous
- **[Lack of Interpretive Unity]** Because it relies on metaphorical definitions, the doctrine lacks a consistent framework
    - Different proponents often hold vastly different views on how the end times are understood
    - It tends to favor individualistic or teacher-dependent interpretations rather than a cohesive biblical model

### The Theological Roots of Amillennialism

- **[The Displacement of Israel]** A core issue with amillennialism is the idea that Israel no longer holds a unique, covenantal place in God's purposes.
    - It suggests Israel has been "delegitimized."
    - It posits that Israel's specific covenant identity and privileges have been transferred to the Church, effectively sweeping the national, ethnic, and territorial aspects of Israel aside.
    - This view argues that because of this transfer, a literal millennium (involving a national/territorial Israel) is no longer necessary.
- **[Subjectivity in Interpretation]** Because the doctrine lacks a consistent rule of measurement, it can lead to highly individualistic interpretations:
    - It allows for "playing fast and loose" with Scripture.
    - It grants individuals the freedom to interpret text however and whenever they want.
- **[Intellect vs. Scripture]** The speaker argues that amillennialism is a system predicated on human intellect rather than the principle of Scripture interpreting Scripture.
    - While believers can be good at comparing different parts of the Bible, the danger lies in failing to let the Scripture speak for itself.

### The Problem of Metaphorical Reinterpretation

- **[Redefining Geography]** Amillennialists often attempt to resolve apparent contradictions in prophecy by redefining physical locations as spiritual symbols.
    - For example, passages in Isaiah (chapters 2, 4, 42, 49, and 6) describe the Messiah returning to Jerusalem to set up His throne and judge the nations.
    - When these texts describe nations bringing gifts to the Messiah in Jerusalem, amillennialists argue:
        - "Jerusalem" refers to the Church.
        - "Zion" represents the covenant people of God (the Church).
        - The "nations of the earth" are being taught righteousness by the Church.
- **[Lack of Scriptural Precedent]** The speaker argues this reinterpretation is arbitrary and lacks linguistic support.
    - Throughout all of Scripture, when Jerusalem is mentioned, it refers to the actual city of Jerusalem.
    - There is no biblical basis for establishing that "Jerusalem" is a consistent metaphor for the Church.

### The Consequences of Amillennialism

- **[Spiritual and Psychological Impact]** The speaker argues that the doctrine is "very destructive for the mind and heart" of the global body of Christ.
    - It causes a "divestment" of both natural and covenantal Israel.
    - This results in an "arrogant taking" of Israel's identities and privileges by the Church.
- **[Theological "Identity Theft"]** The speaker characterizes the replacement of Israel with the Church as a form of "identity theft in the most gross way."
    - The Church attempts to claim the name, historical legacy, and future assurances that were originally promised to Israel.
- **[Obscuring the Return of Christ]** This doctrine skews the Church's understanding of the Second Coming.
    - It fails to account for the physical reality that when the Lord returns, He is coming to a specific place: Jerusalem.
    - **[The Achilles' Heel]** If heaven, Israel, and territory are no longer seen as significant, the literal fulfillment of God's promises to Israel is rendered meaningless.

### The Meaning of a "New Jerusalem"

- The term "new" in prophetic contexts refers to **regeneration and restoration** rather than total replacement.
    - It is not a "new" Jerusalem that has no connectivity to the old one.
    - Instead, it signifies the restoration of all things, similar to how an individual is made "new" in Christ.
- **[Restoration vs. Destruction]** The speaker emphasizes that God's plan is to restore and recover Israel, not to destroy it and replace it with something else.

### Critiques of Catholic Eschatology

- **[The Assumption of Replacement]** Catholic eschatology is built on the premise that the Catholic Church serves as the "new and true Israel."
- **[Historical Continuity]** The speaker notes that despite the Protestant Reformation, many Protestant views remained tethered to Catholic eschatology and ecclesiology (the study of the nature and structure of the Church).

### The Limitations of the Reformation

- **[Failure to Break from Anti-Judaism]** The speaker argues that while the Reformers made significant strides, they failed to shake free from the church's historical anti-Semitism and anti-Judaism.
    - This failure resulted in the continued embrace of the false teaching that Israel has no role in God's plan.
    - This error directly feeds into a false inclusiology regarding the nature, appearance, and function of the Church.
- **[The Irony of the Reformed Church]** Ironically, the speaker notes that the closest resemblance to the Catholic Church today is the Reformed Church.
    - This is because the Reformed tradition was unable to fully break free from Catholic ecclesiology (the study of the nature and structure of the Church) and Catholic eschatology.
- **[The Standard of Scripture]** The speaker emphasizes that the goal is not to demonize Catholicism, but to prioritize biblical fidelity over tradition.
    - Where church history or personal emotions deviate from the Word, believers must adhere to Scripture and repent of those deviations.

### Submitting Tradition to Scripture

- **[The Priority of the Word]** Regardless of denominational background (Catholic, Protestant, Charismatic, Baptist, etc.), believers must bring their spiritual history and lineage to the foot of the Scripture.
    - Theological conclusions should be based on what Scripture says, rather than personal desires or emotional inclinations.
- **[Repenting of History]** There is a call to repent of relying on church history where it deviates from the biblical text.

### The Key to Interpreting Revelation

- **[Christ as the Interpreter]** The symbolism in Revelation is not meant to be an insurmountable burden because Jesus provides the means for understanding.
- **[The Hermeneutical Key]** The speaker points to the relationship between the seven stars and the seven angels, and the seven lampstands and the seven churches, as a foundational principle for interpreting the book's symbolism.
    - By letting the text speak for itself and following Christ's own explanations, the symbolism becomes clear.
