### Approaching Revelation with Jewish Context

- The necessity of a first-century Jewish perspective
    - Understanding the book requires seeing it from the viewpoint of John and his contemporaries
    - This involves being rooted in Jewish apocalyptic literature
- The role of Old Testament literacy
    - High levels of Old Testament knowledge are required to grasp the many allusions and references throughout the text
    - This literacy allows a reader to navigate the complex worldview presented in the book

### The Question of Jesus's Identity

- **[The Consumerist Jesus]** A common issue in the West is the creation of a "consumer-friendly" presentation of Jesus
    - This version often focuses on basic, comforting truths (e.g., He loves me, He is my Savior, He died for me, He will return)
    - **[The Problem]** This simplified view does not square well with the actual Jesus of Scripture
- The central question for this study is: "Who is Jesus?"

### Context of Revelation 1:9-20

- John's experience on the island of Patmos
    - John is beholding his best friend in a state of resurrected glory
    - This is a stark contrast to the last time he saw Him
- **The contrast of Jesus's appearances**
    - The brutal reality of the crucifixion: His body was mangled, ripped apart on a Roman cross, and laid in a borrowed grave
    - The glory of the resurrection: The powerful, resurrected state revealed to John in the vision

### The Manifestations of Jesus

- **[The Distinction of Glory]** There is a difference between the resurrected body and the full glory seen on Patmos
    - The post-resurrection body was a kind of resurrected state, but not yet the full glory and power revealed in the vision
- **A Relational Framework**
    - To understand Revelation, one must view it through John's personal relationship with Jesus
    - John is beholding his best friend, a man he walked with on the shores of Galilee and the Golan Heights
- **Same Person, Different Manifestation**
    - The Jesus John sees on Patmos is the same person he knew in Galilee
    - However, it is a different manifestation of that same person—one that John had not yet connected with during their earthly ministry

### Knowing Jesus in His Fullness

- **[The Limitation of Current Knowledge]** Knowing a specific facet or season of Jesus's manifestation does not mean knowing Him in His entirety
    - A person can know Jesus's earthly ministry and His resurrected glory without knowing His full, absolute nature
    - Full knowledge of His being is deferred until the age to come, when believers receive resurrected bodies and know Him as He knows them
- **The Heart of the Gospel**
    - The core message of the kingdom is the preaching of the "unsearchable riches of Christ"
- **The Role of the Holy Spirit**
    - The Holy Spirit's purpose is to reveal the depths of God to those redeemed by the blood of the Lamb
    - **[The Mind of Christ]** As Paul states in 1 Corinthians 2, the Spirit enables believers to have the mind of Christ to know God in His depths

### The Revelation of God's Depths

- **[Jesus's Yearning]** In John 17, Jesus prays for His followers to know His glory
    - His heart's desire is that believers would experience His wonder and unsearchable riches
- **Defining the "Depths of God"**
    - The New Testament uses various terms to describe the profound nature of God that the Holy Spirit reveals
    - These include:
        - Unsearchable riches
        - Surpassing riches
        - Glory
        - Excellence
        - Surpassing worth
- **Revelation as a Capstone**
    - The book of Revelation serves as a powerful capstone to the revelation of God's depths as seen in the face of Christ
    - This aligns with Jesus's statement in John 14: "If you've seen me, you've seen the Father"

### Meditating on the Imagery of Revelation 1

- The descriptions in Revelation 1 are powerful symbols of the resurrected, glorious man standing before John
- **The Appearance of Jesus**
    - **Head and hair**: Described as being "white like wool, as white as snow"
    - **Eyes**: Described as "a flame of fire"
        - Since eyes are the windows to the soul, this suggests something profound about His soul, heart, and emotional makeup
    - **Feet**: Described as "fine brass"
        - This carries connotations of strength, power, and authority
        - The comparison to brass "refined in a furnace" implies that His strength and authority have been tested by fire
    - **Voice**: Described as "the sound of many waters"
        - This evokes a sense of overwhelming power and presence

### Symbolic Depth of the Vision

- **The Voice of Jesus**
    - Described as "the sound of many waters"
    - **[Cultural Context]** In a Middle Eastern culture that did not live near massive oceans or raging rivers (the Jordan River being more like a small creek), this sound would be unfamiliar and distinct
    - **[Symbolism of Power]** It represents a sound of power that instills fear, awe, respect, and wonder
    - **[The Voice of Warning]** Like a trumpet or a massive river that could sweep a person away, His voice is authoritative and meant to make one tremble
- **The Depth of Symbolic Language**
    - The descriptions in verses 14 through 17 are densely packed with meaning
    - **[The Danger of Surface-Level Reading]** Simply reading the text (e.g., "His head and hair were white like wool") can lead to trivializing the imagery (like picturing a snowman) if the deeper symbolic weight is not considered

### The Old Testament Roots of Revelation's Imagery

- **[Symbolism over Literalism]** The physical descriptions of Jesus in Revelation 1 are not meant to be taken literally (e.g., He does not literally have cotton-like hair)
    - Instead, these descriptions use language from the Old Testament to describe the nature of God
    - By using this language, the Messiah is being revealed as the same God seen in the Old Testament
- **Connection to Daniel's Prophecies**
    - **Head and hair white like wool**: This is an allusion to the "Ancient of Days" from the book of Daniel
    - This identifies Jesus as the "Son of Man" from Daniel 7, who existed before the Father and inherited the kingdom to give to the Saints
- **Allusions to Ezekiel and Isaiah**
    - The descriptions of His eyes being "flames of fire" draw from the symbolic language found in the books of Ezekiel and Isaiah

### The Mystery of the Godhead Manifested

- **[Summary of the Old Testament]** The descriptions provided by John act as a summary of how the Old Testament describes the Messiah and the Ancient of Days
    - This pulls together the mystery of the Godhead (the Father and the Son)
    - It reveals the Messiah as God incarnate: the depths of God manifested in a human frame
- **Additional Imagery of Christ's Glory**
    - **Feet**: Described as being like "fine brass"
    - **Mouth**: A "double-edged sharp two-edged sword" comes out of His mouth, representing His judgments
    - **Countenance**: His face shines with the strength of the sun
        - Described as being "brighter than the Sun"
        - This level of brightness is so intense it is difficult for human perception to capture

### The Weight of the Encounter

- **[The Intensity of Christ's Glory]** Jesus's countenance is not just bright; it is described as shining with the strength of the sun
    - He is identified as the source of the sun's strength
- **John's Reaction: Falling "as dead"**
    - This describes an overwhelming surge of conflicting emotions: awe, dread, fear, wonder, and adrenaline
    - This reaction is a recurring biblical pattern seen in the Old Testament
    - **[Old Testament Precedents]** When prophets encountered the Lord, the experience was so intense it sapped their physical energy, making it feel as though they were dying
        - Daniel
        - Isaiah
        - Ezekiel
        - Jeremiah

### The Intensity of the Divine Encounter

- **[The Experience of Dread]** The encounter with Jesus's glory causes a sense of dread that pushes a person to the brink of death
    - This does not mean Jesus intends to kill the person
    - Rather, the experience is so fierce and intense that it feels like death itself

### The Lamb of God

- One of the most dominant descriptions of Jesus in the book of Revelation
    - The term "Lamb" is used 29 times throughout the book
    - Key chapters include 5, 8, 12, 13, 14, 16, and 17
- **[The Two Dynamics of the Lamb]** The title operates across two distinct realities:
        - **Prophetic Foreshadowing (Old Testament):** Rooted in the Old Testament sacrificial system
                - During the Exodus and Passover, the lamb was the sacrifice for the nation of Israel
                - The blood on the doorposts allowed the judgment to "pass over" the home
        - **Revealed Reality (New Testament):** The reality of what the Lamb is following the crucifixion
                - Jesus's death on the cross is the fulfillment of the sacrifice
                - **[Expansion of Scope]** While the Old Testament lamb was for the nation of Israel, Jesus's sacrifice is for all the nations of the earth

### The Connection Between Exodus and Revelation

- There are powerful similarities between the book of Exodus and the book of Revelation
    - The Lamb and the blood of the Lamb are connected theologically, historically, and symbolically to the judgments of God
- **[Study Tip]** To grasp the full depth, read through Revelation multiple times, focusing on each of the "five faces" individually

### The Dual Nature of the Lamb

- While often associated with mercy, the Lamb is also the agent of judgment
    - In Revelation 5, the Lion of the tribe of Judah is revealed to be a Lamb standing as though slain
    - The Lamb is the one who tears the seals and releases the judgments
    - This includes the concept of being hidden from the "wrath of the Lamb," highlighting that His role encompasses both mercy and judgment

### Upcoming Themes in Revelation

- **[The Lamb of God]** This is the primary "face" of Jesus throughout the book
- **[Jesus the King]** A theme that may be less obvious or more complex than typically presented
    - Much like the concept of "inconceivable" in *The Princess Bride*, the biblical meaning of Jesus as King might differ significantly from modern, common understandings found in popular worship songs or messages

### The Nature of Jesus's Kingship

- **[Modern vs. Biblical]** Modern Christianity often presents a very spiritualized view of Jesus, whereas the kingship in Revelation is fundamentally different
    - Modern views often see Him as a purely heavenly or spiritual king
    - The biblical reality is a **theocracy**
- **[Characteristics of His Kingdom]** The kingship described in Revelation is:
    - **Theocratic**: It is a system of divine rule
    - **Material & Tangible**: It is not just an abstract concept; it involves real, physical presence
    - **Political & Territorial**: It involves actual governance and authority over physical realms
- **[The Messianic Reign]** The nature of His rule is concrete and authoritative:
    - He will rule and reign from an actual throne in Jerusalem
    - He will exercise authority with an actual scepter, even against the kings of the earth who refuse to submit
- **[The End of Human Systems]** The progression of history leads toward this divine rule rather than human structures
    - Democracy is viewed as a momentary reality rather than the ultimate end
    - Jesus's return establishes a theocracy that He deserves because He paid for it in blood
- **[Distinction from Human Dictatorship]** His kingship is not comparable to human political leaders or dictators (e.g., Saddam Hussein, the King of Jordan, or the King of Thailand); it is an altogether different kind of kingdom

### The Transition of Kingdoms

- **[A Hostile Takeover]** The shift from worldly rule to divine rule is described as a "brutal, plain, hostile takeover" of everything
    - This is not an ethereal or metaphorical change
    - It is not a process of Jesus staying in heaven while ruling from afar
- **[The Ultimate End State]** The culmination of the book of Revelation is the Father and the Son dwelling on the earth
    - This existence occurs within a theocracy
    - It is described as lasting for "endless ages"
- **[Scriptural Basis]** The transition is marked by the kingdoms of the world becoming the kingdom of God and Christ (as seen in Revelation 11 and the seventh trumpet)

### Jesus as the Judge

- **[The Third Dominant Face]** Beyond the Lamb and the King, Jesus is prominently presented as the Judge
- **[The Paradox of Judgment]** An interesting tension exists in how His judgments are delivered
    - In many judgment passages, He is described as the Lamb performing the act of judging and releasing judgments

### The Role of Judgment in Revelation

- **[Prevalence]** A significant portion of the book of Revelation is dedicated to judgment events
    - Most of the book can be characterized by passages describing the judgments of God
- **[The Modern Disconnect]** There is a profound tension between contemporary views and biblical reality
    - Modern generations often view the concept of divine judgment as archaic, barbaric, foolish, or offensive
    - This leads to a deep-seated hatred of the concept of judgment
- **[Judgment as the Foundation of Justice]** Despite the aversion, judgment is presented as a necessity
    - The judgments of God are described as the only hope for true justice in the world
    - **[The Human Paradox]** There is a fundamental hypocrisy in the human condition
        - Humans possess a massive thirst for justice
        - Yet, humans reject the very concept of a God who would execute the judgments required to bring that justice about

### The Purpose and Perception of Judgment

- **[The Conflict of the End Age]** The approaching end-of-age scenario is expected to expose a global hatred of God's judgment
    - This includes a hatred of God intervening in human history
    - It includes a resentment toward God causing perceived pain for the purpose of establishing righteousness
- **[Judgment Produces Righteousness]** Contrary to modern views, judgment has a constructive purpose
    - As Isaiah noted, through God's judgment on the earth, the inhabitants learn righteousness
    - Judgment is the means by which righteousness is produced
- **[The Obsolescence of Judgment in Modern Thought]** In many contemporary circles, the judgments of God are viewed as obsolete
    - This is driven by an emphasis on God solely as a savior, merciful, gentle, and a provider of a "gospel of grace"
    - This perspective seeks to separate God's character from His role as a judge
- **[The Question of the Cross]** A central theological question arises: Did Jesus bear the full weight of God's judgments on the cross?
    - The speaker begins to address whether the judgments are truly obsolete in light of the crucifixion

### The Relationship Between the Cross and Divine Judgment

- **[The Delusion of Obsolescence]** There is a persistent, destructive teaching within some charismatic circles regarding the nature of judgment
    - This teaching suggests that because Jesus bore the judgments of God on the cross, God's role as a judge has become obsolete
    - The speaker characterizes this view as "delusional" and "destructive"
    - **[The Consequence]** This perspective sets the Body of Christ up for "profound offensive disillusionment" when the reality of judgment is eventually encountered
- **[The Harmony of Lion and Lamb]** Jesus's role as judge does not contradict His identity as the Savior
    - When Jesus executes judgment, He is not betraying His nature as the man of God
    - He does not violate the "Lamb of God" within Him even as He "roars like a furious lion"
    - His judgments and His mercy are part of a single, diversely excellent character
- **[The Coming Calibration]** As the return of the Lord approaches, a shift is expected in the global Body of Christ
    - The Lord is expected to "calibrate" the reality of the Church regarding these truths over the coming decade

### Jesus as the Husband and Bridegroom

- **[A Dominant Identity]** Beyond being the King, Judge, Lamb, Messiah, or Son of God, a fourth, highly dominant face of Jesus in Revelation is the Husband or Bridegroom
    - This identity emphasizes a deep, covenantal relationship rather than just a political or legal one
- **[The Purpose of the Coming Kingdom]** The framework for understanding the coming king and His kingdom is rooted in this relationship
    - According to Matthew 22, the kingdom of heaven is likened to a father arranging a wedding for his son
    - **[The Mystery of Marriage]** Marriage was created as a pointer to the type of intimate, known, and related relationship that Jesus desires to have with believers
- **[The Conclusion of Revelation]** The book of Revelation concludes with the bride being made ready for her husband, highlighting the importance of this covenantal union

### The Scriptural Reality of God as Husband

- **[A Relational Identity]** God's desire to be known is not limited to roles like Judge or Savior; He explicitly seeks to be known as a Husband
    - **Hosea 2**: God promises a shift in how His people address Him, moving from titles like "Master" to the intimate "my husband"
    - **John the Baptist**: Identified himself as a "friend of the bridegroom"
    - **Paul**: Expresses the concept of being betrothed to "one husband"
- **[The Emotional Weight of Covenant]** The prophets used the imagery of broken or absent marriage to convey the profound pain of a fractured relationship with God
    - **Ezekiel**: Experienced the loss of a wife before the judgments of Babylon to understand and convey God's own grief over His covenant people
    - **Jeremiah**: Was forbidden from marrying to serve as a living sign of the loneliness and abandonment felt when a covenant partner wanders away

### The Centrality of the Covenantal Marriage

- **[A Dominant Reality]** The identity of God as Husband is not a "side fringe reality" but a dominant facet of His character
    - Isaiah repeatedly conveys the message of the Maker being the Husband of His people
- **[The Mystery of Marriage]** The concept of marriage is a pointer to a much larger spiritual reality
    - It is not primarily about human husbands and wives
    - It is about the covenant union between Christ and the Church
    - It represents the mystery of "two becoming one" within a divine relationship

### Distinguishing Relational Identities

- **[Jesus as Savior]** While the concept of Jesus as a Savior is true and vital, it functions differently in a relational context
    - He is a Savior who saves, is saving, and will save
    - However, the term "Savior" is often used in worship in a way that doesn't always capture the same relational depth as the imagery of the Husband

### Contemporary Language vs. Scriptural Terminology

- **[The Language Gap]** Much of the terminology used in modern preaching, teaching, worship songs, and art is rooted in contemporary culture rather than strict scriptural revelation
    - It is rare to hear worship songs focusing on Jesus as the **Covenant Husband**
    - It is uncommon to hear Him described as the **Judge** or the **Theocratic King**
- **[The Fullness of the Lamb]** Modern depictions of the "Lamb of God" often focus exclusively on the sacrificial aspect of the cross, potentially obscuring His scriptural role in judgment
    - In scripture, the Lamb is also the one who releases judgments and possesses wrath
- **[The Importance of Biblical Beholding]** A primary goal for believers should be to know the specific "faces" of Jesus as He intended to be revealed in the Bible
    - Beholding an incomplete or non-biblical version of Jesus (e.g., a version that no longer judges) leads to a life that does not accurately reflect His true nature

### Jesus as the Military Leader

- **[The Root of David and Jesse]** This identity is a recurring reality throughout the book of Revelation
    - While the specific phrase may not appear in every passage, the underlying reality is present in every chapter
- **[The Nature of His Kingship]** Being a king in the biblical sense involves more than just sitting on a throne
    - Historical kings, like David, were active military leaders involved in war and conquest
    - Jesus is described as having a scepter (Revelation 2 and 12) and a throne, signifying His authority as a leader who knows war
    - This identity emphasizes His role in military conquest rather than just a passive or purely spiritual reign

### Jesus as a Man of War

- **[The Peak of His Identity]** Revelation 19 represents the highest point of this military reality
    - The text describes one coming on a white horse, stained in the blood of nations and equipped with weapons
    - This is not a metaphor to be "danced away," but a literal description of Jesus as a military leader
- **[Biblical Precedent]** This identity is consistent with the character of God seen in other scriptures
    - In the book of Exodus, God is described as a man of war
    - This same dynamic is present in the book of Revelation
- **[The Gap in Modern Understanding]** There is a significant lack of awareness regarding this aspect of Jesus in the Western church
    - This identity has been "criminally underdeveloped" in modern teaching
    - Many in the church live in an alternate reality that lacks consciousness of Jesus's zeal for holy war

### The Believer's Path in the Present Age

- **[The Nature of Holy War]** The holy war mentioned in Revelation is not something for humans to execute on His behalf through militancy
        - It is a war that Jesus Himself will execute upon His return
- **[The Way of the Cross]** Believers are called to walk as "lambs led to the slaughter" in this current age
        - This means following the model of the crucified lamb
        - By "going low" through the way of the cross, believers are promised to be "exalted high" in the age to come

- **[The Danger of Presuppositions]** Bringing preconceived notions to the study of Revelation creates a profound disconnect from reality
    - If one enters the text assuming certain aspects (like judgment) are merely allegorical, they will fail to interact with the actual message
    - These contradictions prevent a person from understanding the "depths of the riches" intended by the book
- **[The Need for Theological Reformatting]** To truly know Jesus as He is, one must be willing to undergo a mental and spiritual overhaul
    - The speaker suggests the need to "wipe the hard drive" of one's theological brain
    - The goal is to allow the book of Revelation to "reformat the hard drives of our hearts and our minds"
- **[Knowing Him as He Is]** The purpose of this study is to move beyond how we *think* He is or how we *want* Him to be
    - We must seek a revelation of Jesus as He is actually revealed in Scripture
    - This preparation is essential for the day we will see Him in His full glory upon His return
