### Interpretations of Second Thessalonians Chapter 2

- Speculation regarding the timing of the Day of the Lord and the Great Tribulation
    - Some interpretations suggested a return appearing as early as October 22, 1992
    - Such a timeline would imply being in the Great Tribulation (lasting three and a half years) and under the Antichrist's reign of terror
- Historical patterns of belief in imminent returns
    - Thousands of people believed Jesus was coming in 1988 due to the circulation of a popular book

### Refuting Imminent Return Claims

- The distinction between a "secret rapture" and the "glorious return" of Christ
    - If the return is the glorious appearing in the sky (as suggested by some brochures), it implies the Great Tribulation is already underway
- Evidence that the Day of the Lord has not yet arrived
    - The necessary precursors have not been observed:
        - The unfolding of the seven seals
        - The seven trumpet judgments
        - The rise of the Antichrist's empire, career, and power (which lasts 3.5 years or 42 months)
- Historical parallels in the Thessalonian church
    - The original Thessalonians faced similar misinformation
    - They were told the Day of the Lord had already come, leading to confusion over whether they had missed a rapture or if the rapture simply did not exist

### Deception Tactics in the Thessalonian Church

- The use of false authority to spread contradictory teachings
    - False teachers claimed to have received a "new revelation" from a spirit
    - They presented this as a new message or letter that supposedly superseded or updated Paul's teachings
- Forgery and the importance of Paul's handwriting
    - Because Paul often dictated his letters, deceivers could pass off a forged letter as his work
    - Paul used a personal signature to combat this:
        - In 2 Thessalonians 3:17, he explicitly states he writes with his own hand
        - This served as a distinguishing mark to validate his authentic letters and expose fakes

### The Impact of Misinformation

- The psychological and spiritual effect on the believers
    - The false claims "shook them from their composure" and caused deep disturbance
    - This confusion stemmed from a specific theological assumption:
        - They believed they would be raptured *before* the Day of the Lord began
        - Therefore, being told the Day of the Lord had already arrived implied they had missed the rapture
- The fear of divine wrath
    - The misinformation led to intense agitation and fear
    - Believers felt they were about to be caught unprepared in the "Day of the Lord," which is characterized as a period of fierce and final wrath from God

### Paul's Response to Anxiety and Deception

- Paul addresses the confusion caused by fearful messages (like the idea that believers might have missed the rapture or are already in the tribulation)
- His approach involves laying out specific teachings from 2 Thessalonians 2:3 through 2:15
- **The primary warning: "Let no one in any way deceive you" (v. 3)**
    - Deception regarding these topics can create intense fear and anxiety
    - Specific areas of potential deception include:
        - Claims that the Great Tribulation has already begun
        - Claims that the Day of the Lord is currently underway
        - Any errors or misinformation regarding the return of Jesus Christ
- **A directive to resist "new revelations"**
    - Believers are cautioned not to be swayed by claims of new messages that contradict established teaching

### Remembering Established Teachings

- **[The directive to not be forgetful]** Paul reminds believers to rely on the truths they have already been taught rather than being swayed by new, sensational claims
    - This serves as a defense against people claiming personal revelations (e.g., "God told me it is October 22")
    - Believers are encouraged to measure new messages against the known word of God and the teachings of true apostles
- **The required sequence of events (2 Thessalonians 2:3-5)**
    - Before the Day of the Lord can occur, certain events must take place first:

        1. The apostasy must occur
        2. The man of lawlessness (the Antichrist) must be revealed

- **Characteristics of the Antichrist**
    - Described as the "son of destruction"
    - He opposes and exalts himself above every so-called god or object of worship
    - His ultimate act is taking his seat in the temple of God, displaying himself as being God
    - This event is also referred to as the "abomination of desolation" (Daniel 9; Matthew 24)
- **Conclusion regarding imminent dates**
    - Because the Antichrist has not yet appeared or desecrated the temple, it is impossible to be in the latter half of the Great Tribulation or the Day of the Lord

### Avoiding Ignorance

- **[The third directive]** Paul instructs believers not to be ignorant to prevent them from being waylaid by fear
- **The restraining force (2 Thessalonians 2:6)**
    - There is currently a force holding back the Antichrist
    - Satan desires to inhabit a person to carry out the great desecration, but he is being restrained
- **The 'Mystery of Lawlessness' (v. 7)**
    - The wicked intent and antichrist activity are already at work in the world
    - While many people exhibit an "antichrist attitude," the ultimate embodiment of lawlessness has not yet arrived
- **The identity of the Restrainer**
    - The Holy Spirit is the force currently restraining Satan from bringing about his ultimate false Christ and attempting world domination
    - The Antichrist cannot appear until this restrainer is removed in God's timing

### The Power and Destruction of the Antichrist

- **The Antichrist's ultimate end (2 Thessalonians 2:8)**
    - The Lord will destroy him and his entire enterprise
    - This destruction occurs through the "breath of his mouth" at the appearance of the Lord's coming
- **The nature of his power (2 Thessalonians 2:9-10)**
    - He possesses the power to perform signs and false wonders
    - He can muster all the deception that wickedness is capable of producing
- **The scope of his influence**
    - His influence is specifically directed over "those who perish"
    - This refers to the ungodly people of the world
- **[Why this matters]** Because his rise will involve massive, global deception via miracles and signs, it will not be an obscure or isolated event; it will be unmistakable

### Avoiding Ignorance and Fear

- Paul's goal is to prevent believers from being ignorant of these future details
- Knowing these precursors—such as the apostasy and the Antichrist's desecration of the temple—acts as a safeguard against:
    - Being deceived by false revelations
    - Forgetting established biblical teachings
    - Living in fear of the Second Coming

### The Warning Against Unbelief

- **[The fourth directive]** Paul warns the Thessalonians not to be "unbelieving"
    - This serves as a specific warning for anyone in the church who is not a true Christian or believer
    - Even if one avoids deception, forgetfulness, and ignorance, they may still lack faith
- **The reason for perishing (2 Thessalonians 2:10)**
    - Those who perish (the lost or damned) are characterized by one specific failure:
        - They "did not receive the love of the truth so as to be saved"
    - **[Why this matters]** This highlights that perishing is a matter of human choice and an unwillingness to accept the truth, rather than just a lack of information

### The Role of Truth in Salvation

- Salvation is tied to the active reception of truth
- Rejection of truth is the mechanism by which people are set for eternal punishment
- This concept is reinforced by Jesus' words in John 5:39 regarding the importance of searching the Scriptures

### The Moral Nature of Unbelief

- **[The core issue]** Resistance to the gospel is not an intellectual problem, but a moral one
    - People often possess the necessary information (the Scriptures) but lack the desire to act on it
    - This is described as a "moral antipathy" to the truth
- **The distinction between information and volition**
    - As seen in John 5:39-40, individuals may search the Scriptures and find evidence of Christ, yet remain "unwilling to come" to Him
    - **[Why this matters]** It shifts the focus from a lack of evidence to the state of the human heart and will
- **The consequence of unbelief (John 8:24)**
    - Jesus states that without belief in Him, people "shall die in [their] sins"
    - Dying in one's sins means the sins have not been atoned for or covered
    - **[The mechanism of perishing]** People perish because they are "unwilling to believe"; it is ultimately a question of human volition

### The Moral Dimension of Faith

- **[Intellectual vs. Moral]** Understanding the gospel is not the same as believing it
    - Many people can intellectually appreciate the "data" of the gospel: a God who loves, forgives, and offers peace
    - However, the actual decision to follow Christ is a moral one, not an intellectual one
- **The core dilemma**
    - The choice is not between belief and unbelief in a vacuum, but between two loves:
        - The love of the truth
        - The love of one's sin
    - **[Why this matters]** True faith requires the willingness to abandon sin, repent, and turn toward righteousness
- **The root of resistance (John 3:19)**
    - People do not reject the gospel because they lack information
    - They reject it because of a moral antipathy to truth
    - As stated in John 3:19, "men love darkness rather than light because their deeds are evil"

### Receiving the Love of the Truth

- **[The distinction in 2 Thessalonians 2:10]** People perish because they did not receive the "love of the truth" so as to be saved
    - The text specifies that the failure is not in receiving the truth itself, but in failing to receive the *love* of it
    - **[What this implies]** This suggests a lack of desire for salvation; they loved their sin more than they loved the truth
- **Defining "the truth"**
    - Primarily, the truth is the gospel of the Lord Jesus Christ (the saving truth)
    - It can also refer to Christ himself as the embodiment of that truth
    - **[Biblical connection]** This aligns with 1 Corinthians 16:22, which states that anyone who does not love the Lord Jesus Christ is accursed
- **The moral choice**
    - Coming to Christ is a moral decision that requires a person to say: "I will no longer love my sin; I will love Christ"

### Protection from Deception

- **[The scope of Antichrist's deception]** The horrible deceptions of the Antichrist are directed at "perishing people"
    - These are individuals who do not love the truth so as to be saved
    - **[Why they are vulnerable]** Because they do not love the truth, they are prone to love and believe a lie
- **[The believer's security]** If one is not unbelieving, they have nothing to fear from the Antichrist's coming

### The Reality for the Unbeliever

- **[Two sources of fear]** An unbelieving person faces two distinct fears:

    1. The return of Christ in glory, which comes in judgment against all ungodliness
    2. The coming of the Antichrist, who will delude and deceive with wickedness

- **Defining the Unbeliever**
    - Does not know God
    - Does not obey the gospel of our Lord Jesus
    - Does not love the truth of Christ and His atoning work
    - **[The nature of their choice]** They willfully choose to love their sin, the lies of Satan, and themselves over the gospel and Christ

### Intellectual Apprehension vs. Loving the Truth

- **[The distinction]** It is possible to receive the truth intellectually without actually loving it
    - One can grasp the facts of the gospel without a heart-level commitment
- **Example: The Rulers in John 12:42**
    - Many rulers believed in Jesus intellectually
    - **[The barrier]** They refused to confess Him because they feared being put out of the synagogue
    - **[The root cause]** They loved the approval of men more than the approval of God
- **The essence of discipleship**
    - Becoming a Christian is a matter of what one loves most
    - It requires loving God, Christ, the gospel, and God's approval above all else
    - **[The conflict]** It is a struggle between loving righteousness and hating sin
- **Why the perishing are deceived**
    - They may hear, understand, or even affirm that the truth is true
    - **[The failure]** They perish because they do not love Christ and instead prefer to love their sin and lies

### The Moral Basis of Unbelief

- **[Not an intellectual failure]** The inability to see the truth is not a question of the mind or thinking capacity
    - It is fundamentally a question of morality
- **[The connection between sin and lies]** To love sin, one must necessarily believe lies
    - To maintain a love for sin, a person must buy into the lie that sin is beneficial

### The Divine Consequence of Willful Unbelief

- **[Divine Recompense]** Because unbelief is a willful refusal to love and obey the truth, it carries severe consequences
- **[God's Deluding Influence]** As described in 2 Thessalonians 2:11, God will act upon unbelievers to seal their fate
    - God sends a "deluding influence" upon them
    - This influence causes them to believe what is false
    - This is presented as a sovereign act of divine judgment against those who have willfully rejected the truth

### The Judicial Nature of the Deluding Influence

- **[A force of deception]** The "deluding influence" acts as a literal force of delusion or deception
    - It ensures that those who have willfully rejected the truth will believe what is false
- **Connection to the Antichrist**
    - According to the Book of Revelation, the whole world will worship the Antichrist
    - **[The reason for mass deception]** This occurs because God has sent a deluding force upon those who were willfully unbelieving, causing them to believe the lie
    - While the Antichrist performs mighty signs and wonders, the fundamental reason for people being "sucked into Satan's lie" is this divine, judicial influence
- **The principle of judicial hardening**
    - God judicially responds to the choice to believe a lie by hardening the individual so they cannot believe the truth even if they hear it
    - **Example: Pharaoh in Exodus**
        - Initially, Pharaoh repeatedly hardened his own heart in response to God's commands
        - Eventually, the text shows that God hardened Pharaoh's heart (Exodus 9, 12)
        - **[The progression]** Pharaoh chose a path, and God then fixed him in that path, making it impossible to turn back

### The Sealing of Unbelief

- **[The chains of sin]** According to Proverbs 5:22, a person's own iniquities serve as a trap
    - The wicked are held captive by the "chains of [their] own sin"
- **[Divine sealing]** God can seal individuals in their state of unbelief, making their spiritual condition fixed

### The Insensitivity of the Heart

- **[The Isaiah 6 Prophecy]** God instructs Isaiah to preach, but notes a specific divine consequence for the people
    - God commands the preaching of repentance, righteousness, and judgment
    - **[The hardening effect]** As a response to the people, God describes a process of spiritual dulling:
        - Hearts become insensitive
        - Ears become dull
        - Eyes become dim
        - **[The purpose]** This prevents them from seeing, hearing, or understanding with their hearts, which would otherwise allow them to "return to be healed"
- **[New Testament fulfillment]** This prophetic pattern of spiritual blindness is repeated in the New Testament
    - **Jesus and the Parables (Matthew 13:13)**
        - Jesus explains that he speaks in parables because of this phenomenon
        - While the people are seeing and hearing, they are not truly perceiving or understanding
        - This is presented as the fulfillment of the Isaiah prophecy: the people's hearts have become dull, and they have closed their eyes to avoid the healing that comes through true understanding

### The Judicial Consequence of Rejecting Truth

- **[The transition from choice to consequence]** If a person repeatedly rejects the truth, a point is reached where they can no longer hear or see it
    - This is a recurring biblical theme found in:
        - Mark 4:12
        - Luke 8:10
        - John 12:40
        - Acts 28:26-27
- **[The nature of divine judgment]** Judgment can manifest as a "deluding influence"
    - God sentences the willful unbeliever to accept evil as good and lies as truth
    - They have chosen falsehood, and God allows them to receive nothing more than that
- **[Deception as an instrument of judgment]** God uses the forces of Satan and the Antichrist to carry out this punishment
    - These figures serve as the means by which the "perishing" (those who refused to love Christ) are punished
    - **Example: 1 Kings 22:23**
        - In the Old Testament, God used a deceiving spirit as a tool for His own purposes
        - This illustrates that God can use deceptive forces to respond to disobedience and unfaithfulness

### The Romans 1 Pattern of Judicial Response

- **[The distinction between knowledge and will]** Romans 1 illustrates that humans can possess intellectual awareness of God without having the moral will to submit to Him
    - **Knowledge through creation and conscience (Romans 1:19-20)**
        - God is evident within humanity
        - His existence is revealed through the creation and the human conscience
    - **The indictment (Romans 1:21)**
        - Despite having this knowledge, people did not honor Him as God
        - **[The consequence]** This failure of the moral will leads to a darkening of the heart and foolishness, despite intellectual capacity
- **[The concept of being "given over"]** When humanity persistently chooses to reject God, a judicial transition occurs
    - **Passing the point of grace (Romans 1:24, 26, 28)**
        - Because people did not see fit to acknowledge God, He "gave them over" to their own chosen desires
        - This represents a state where the individual has passed the point of grace and is left to the consequences of their own moral decisions

### The Transition from Grace to Judgment

- **[Passing the point of grace]** There is a threshold where divine striving ends and judicial response begins
    - **Genesis 6 Parallel**
        - Just as God declared His spirit would not always strive with man, there comes a point where He stops striving and begins judging
    - **The consequence of persistent sin**
        - God "lets go" and turns individuals over to the consequences of their own moral choices
        - **Romans 1 examples of being "given over"**
            - To degrading passions (Romans 1:26)
            - To a depraved mind (Romans 1:28)
            - To lusts (Romans 1:24)
- **[The state of the hardened]** Once a person is in the period of judgment, their capacity for truth is lost
    - They become fixed and hardened in their chosen path
    - They reach a state where they have no capacity to believe anything but a lie

### The Ultimate Deception: The Antichrist

- **[The central lie]** The core deception during the end times is the belief that the Antichrist is God
    - People will be led to believe the Antichrist is God incarnate—the true Messiah and Savior
- **[The targets of deception]** This deception will have massive global and religious impact
    - **Israel**
        - Because they are still looking for a Messiah, they may be susceptible to believing the Antichrist is the one they have been waiting for
    - **The Nations**
        - The world at large will "buy into" the claim that the Antichrist is God and Christ
- **[Why the deception succeeds]** This is not a failure of information, but a confirmation of character
    - Those who fall for it are confirmed in their belief because they have already willfully rejected the "love of the truth"

### The State of Perishing Man

- **[The condition of separation]** To perish is to be in a state of active enmity with God
    - The individual is under judgment and condemned to an eternal loss
- **[The refusal of grace]** Perishing is not caused by a lack of opportunity for reconciliation
    - Only grace through the sacrificial death of God's Son can bridge the gap to Him
    - Perishing individuals do not just 'presume' on grace; they actively refuse it because they prefer sin
- **[Moral vs. Intellectual failure]** Unbelief is a matter of the heart, not the mind
    - It is not that the truth is not understandable or believable
    - It is a moral decision to love sin over the truth and the Christ of the truth

### The Cycle of Judicial Sealing

- **[Self-imposed consequences]** Willful unbelief creates a set condition that seals a person in their own iniquity
    - **The progression of hardening**
        - Refusing light leads to receiving darkness (and the inability to recognize light)
        - Hardening the heart leads to being permanently hardened
        - Refusing the love of truth leads to receiving a lying spirit
- **[The ultimate outcome]** The judicial response to rejecting eternal life is the reception of eternal death
    - God may use even Satan and the Antichrist as instruments of punishment for those who have rejected Him

### The Irreversibility of Persistent Sin

- **[The danger of delay]** There is a window of opportunity for repentance that, if missed, leads to a fixed state
    - Perishing people reject the gospel and refuse to love Jesus Christ
    - Because they love sin, they follow Satan instead of God
    - Unless they repent while they still can, they eventually reach a point where they are unable to change their pattern

### Paul's Fifth Directive: Don't Be Insecure

- **[The basis for confidence]** Paul provides a way for believers to avoid anxiety by looking at their standing in God
- **[A mini-soteriology]** Based on 2 Thessalonians 1:13-14, the process of salvation is outlined:
    - **God's Choice**: God has chosen believers from the beginning for salvation
    - **The Means**: Salvation occurs through sanctification by the Spirit and faith in the truth
    - **The Purpose**: Believers are called through the gospel so they may gain the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ

### Overcoming Fear through Divine Election

- **[The scope of redemptive security]** 2 Thessalonians 1:13-14 offers a vast foundation for believer confidence, ensuring that the timing of future events does not cause anxiety
    - **No fear of missing the rapture**: Believers do not need to worry about being left out or caught up in the Day of the Lord
    - **No fear of forfeiting blessedness**: Because God has chosen believers for glory, they cannot accidentally lose their future standing or face His fury

### The Tension of Eternal Security

- **[The concern of moral apathy]** A common critique of the belief in eternal salvation is that it might encourage a lifestyle of laziness or disobedience
    - If people believe salvation is permanent, they might feel they have nothing to hold onto
    - This leads to the fear that doctrine could cause individuals to become indifferent to God's commands

### The Security of Eternal Salvation

- **[A complete redemptive arc]** Salvation is not a momentary event but a continuous process that spans all of time:
    - **Origin**: It began with the love of God in eternity past
    - **Process**: It involves being set apart by the Holy Spirit and exercising faith in the truth
    - **End Goal**: It concludes in the believer's glorification, where they gain the glory of the Lord Jesus Christ
- **[The motive for obedience]** There is a vital distinction between working *for* salvation and working *because* of salvation:
    - **Fear-based obedience**: Working and obeying in an attempt to "keep" or maintain salvation
    - **Gratitude-based obedience**: Seeking to obey as a way to thank the God who keeps one's salvation
- **[The certainty of God's keeping]** Believers can have confidence that they will not "fall through the cracks" of God's plan
    - This is supported by the principle in John 6 that all the Father gives to Jesus will come to Him, and He will lose none of them

### The Contrast of Salvation and Damnation

- **[The divergence of outcomes]** There is a sharp contrast between the state of those being judged and those being glorified:
    - **Those being judged**: Refuse to believe, refuse to love Christ, and refuse to obey the truth
    - **Those being glorified**: Receive the blessings of Christ rather than the horrors of the antichrist
    - **The nature of truth**: While some are given a satanic delusion, believers are given the truth

### The Fundamental Distinction of Choice

- **[The direction of response]** A key principle to understand the majesty of salvation is the direction in which the response flows:
    - **In damnation**: God acts in response to human choice
    - **In salvation**: Man acts in response to divine choice

### The Security of the Elect

- **[A promise of glory]** Those chosen by God have no reason to fear the coming judgments
    - While God sends a deluding influence to those who refuse to love the truth, the elect are promised the glory to which they were chosen
- **[The foundation of security]** Confidence is found in the eternal nature of God's plan
    - Security is possible because God planned salvation before the foundation of the world
    - This prevents the "insecurity" that comes from trying to maintain salvation through works

### Summary of Paul's Directives for the Believer

To avoid fear and remain steadfast, the believer must navigate several spiritual pitfalls:

- **Avoid Deception**: Do not be misled by false claims regarding the timing of Christ's return
- **Avoid Forgetfulness**: Stay grounded in established biblical teachings
- **Avoid Ignorance**: Seek a full understanding of the truths of Scripture
- **Avoid Unbelief**: Maintain a heart that loves and responds to the truth
- **Avoid Insecurity**: Trust in the unchanging nature of God's redemptive work

### The Hopeful Anticipation of Christ's Return

- **[The purpose of the return]** For the believer, the return of Christ is not an event to be feared, but a moment of rescue and reunion:
    - To deliver believers from the wrath to come
    - To meet believers in the air
    - To take believers to the place He has prepared for them
- **[The believer's posture]** Instead of anxiety or fear regarding the Day of the Lord, the goal is to:
    - Look forward to the return with great hope
    - Love His appearing
    - Rejoice in the promise of being gathered with Him
