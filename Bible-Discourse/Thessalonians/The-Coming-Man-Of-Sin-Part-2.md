## Study of 2 Thessalonians 2

- The task of unfolding the meaning of God's Word is a sacred trust and duty
    - This process involves "rightly dividing" the Word
    - The Holy Spirit provides the necessary help for this task

### The Antichrist in 2 Thessalonians 2

- The central theme of this chapter is a specific personality known as the Antichrist
    - He is described through several titles in the text:
            - The man of lawlessness (v. 3)
            - The son of destruction (v. 3)
            - The lawless one (v. 8)
- His character and actions are defined by extreme blasphemy and opposition to God
    - He opposes and exalts himself above every so-called god or object of worship
    - He takes his seat in the temple of God, displaying himself as being God (v. 4)
- His arrival and influence are driven by Satanic activity
    - His coming aligns with the activity of Satan (v. 9)
    - He utilizes power, signs, false wonders, and the deception of wickedness to target those who perish
- His ultimate objective
    - He serves as Satan's final earthly blasphemer
    - He attempts to ultimately oppose Jesus Christ and destroy all worship of God

### The Character of the Antichrist

- He is a composite figure representing the culmination of all who hate God and Christ
    - He embodies the peak of all blasphemers, false prophets, false teachers, pseudo-Christs, and hypocrites
    - He serves as the ultimate hypocrite, liar, and deceiver
    - While the "Antichrist spirit" has been present throughout redemptive history, it will reach its peak in this one final individual
- Paul's prior instruction to the Thessalonians
    - This was not new information to the believers
    - In verse 5, Paul reminds them that he had already taught them about the coming of this man during his previous time with them

### The Nature and Origins of the Antichrist

- He represents the culmination of all who hate God and Christ
    - He is the embodiment of all false prophets, false teachers, pseudo-Christs, hypocrites, liars, and deceivers
    - While the "Antichrist spirit" has existed throughout redemptive history, it culminates in this one final individual
- His primary objective is to obliterate the name of God and Christ from the earth
- His arrival is tied to the end times, occurring around the time of the return of Jesus Christ

### Prophetic Connections to Daniel

- Paul's teachings to the Thessalonians likely drew upon the prophecies of Daniel
    - Daniel provides more detail about the Antichrist than any other Old Testament prophet
- **The "Little Horn" (Daniel 7:8)**
    - The Antichrist is described as a "little horn" appearing among other horns
    - This indicates he has associates or works alongside other world rulers
    - He rises from a position of being relatively small to a position of great prominence, eventually becoming larger than his associates
- **The Ten Horns (Daniel 7:7, 24)**
    - Ten horns grow out of the fourth beast, and they are ten kings or kingdoms
    - The fourth beast is the historical Islamic Caliphate and the ten horns are the Revived Islamic Caliphate, matching the feet of iron and clay in Daniel 2
    - The little horn uproots three of the horns and takes charge of all ten
- **Intellectual Character**
    - Daniel 7:8 describes him as having "eyes like the eyes of a man"
    - This suggests he is an intellectual genius—clever, shrewd, and highly intelligent

### Further Prophetic Details from Daniel 7

- **Oratorical and Proud Nature**
    - He is described as having a mouth uttering "great things" or "great boasts"
    - This indicates he is a highly skilled orator who uses his speech to express extreme pride and open blasphemy against God
- **Hostility Toward the Saints**
    - He wages war against the saints and seeks to overpower them
    - His goal is the total destruction of the people of God
    - He wears them out through systematic injustice, including:
        - Seizure of property or rights
        - Punishment
        - Imprisonment
        - Execution
- **Global Dominance and Military Power**
    - He grows to become the dominant power of the world
    - His movement is described as devouring, treading down, and crushing the whole earth
    - This suggests a level of military genius and power surpassing any previous ruler
- **Ultimate Blasphemy**
    - He speaks out against "the Most High"
    - This reinforces his role as an abominator of God's holiness

### Alterations in Times and Laws

- He intends to disrupt the established order of society
    - **Alterations in Times**: Introducing new religious ceremonies and observances to replace traditional celebrations, ultimately centering worship on himself
    - **Alterations in Law**: Changing the world's moral and ethical codes—which were previously founded on God's will—to be satanically inspired
- **Duration of Power**
    - He is granted tremendous power for a specific period
    - This duration is described as "a time, times, and half a time" (three and a half years)

### The Final Judgment and the Everlasting Kingdom

- The Antichrist's dominion is not permanent
    - A court will sit for judgment
    - His dominion will be annihilated and destroyed forever
- **The Triumph of God's Kingdom**
    - Sovereignty and greatness will be transferred to the people of the saints of the Highest One
    - This kingdom will be an everlasting kingdom where all dominions serve and obey

### Additional Characteristics from Daniel 8

- **Insolence and Intrigue**
    - He is described as a king who arises "insolent and skilled in intrigue"
    - **Insolence**: He acts as an intimidator, possessing a "fierce face" that forces others into submission
    - **Intrigue**: He is a master of deception and shrewdness
- **Source of Power**
    - His power is mighty, but it is not his own
    - He is empowered, inspired, and likely indwelt by Satan (as noted in Revelation 13:2)
- **Methods of Destruction**
    - He uses his deceit to magnify himself in his own heart
    - He targets the vulnerable, destroying many "while they are at ease" and killing innocent victims at peace
- **Antiochus as a Type**
    - The little horn of Daniel 8:9 was first fulfilled by Antiochus IV Epiphanes, the Seleucid king, and he is a pattern of the Antichrist
    - Gabriel says the vision is "for the time of the end" (Daniel 8:17-19)
    - The Seleucid-Ptolemaic region of Antiochus is the same region as the Islamic Caliphate, so Daniel 2, 7 and 8 all point to the Antichrist arising from the Middle East
- **The Divine End**
    - He opposes the "Prince of Princes" (the Messiah)
    - **Destruction without human agency**: He cannot be defeated by man
    - He will be crushed by "a stone cut out without hands," a reference to the Messiah's intervention

### The Willful King (Daniel 11)

- Described as a king who does exactly what he pleases
    - He functions as a total, Satan-indwelt dictator of the world
- **Primary Objective: Blasphemy**
    - He seeks to blaspheme God and Christ
    - He aims to destroy all who worship them
    - He exalts and magnifies himself above every other god
- **Nature of his Rule**
    - He uses his absolute, sovereign power to reach unprecedented limits
    - He speaks "monstrous things" against the true God
    - He will be highly effective and "prosper" in his profanity
- **Duration and End**
    - His effectiveness lasts only until the "indignation is finished"
    - He is limited to a period of three and a half years, ending when God's fury is complete
- **Rejection of the God of His Fathers**
    - He shows no regard for "the God of his fathers", which is a reference to Yahweh, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob
    - This is not just a man rejecting the religion of his youth

### Further Characteristics from Daniel 11

- **Disregard for the 'Desire of Women'**
    - This phrase in verse 37 is read as the Messiah, who was known to the Jews as the "desire of women" because every Jewish mother hoped to bear Him (Walvoord, Gaebelein, Miller)
    - It does not mean that the Antichrist is a homosexual
- **Rejection of Every Other God**
    - He will show no concern for any other religion or deity
    - He magnifies himself above them all
    - He still honors a god, so he may demand total allegiance and submission without actually proclaiming himself to be God Almighty
- **The God of War**
    - He honors only a "god of fortresses" or a "god of war"—a deity unknown to his ancestors
    - This foreign god is read as Allah, the moon-and-war god of Arabia
    - He uses immense wealth to fund this deity and his own agenda
        - He uses gold, silver, costly stones, and treasures to finance his power
- **Military and Political Strategy**
    - He is capable of taking action against the strongest fortresses
    - He utilizes foreign gods or allies to achieve military dominance
    - He rewards political loyalty by elevating those who acknowledge him, allowing them to rule over many and parcel out land

### Final Details from Daniel 11

- **Total Control of Land and Authority**
    - He takes control of land to generate wealth
    - He seizes all authority to install his own people into positions of power
- **The Royal Pavilion in Jerusalem**
    - Verse 45 states he will pitch his royal pavilion "between the seas"
        - This refers to the location between the Mediterranean Sea and the Dead Sea
        - This places him in Jerusalem, specifically on the holy mountain
    - He sets himself up in the very location where God has established His temple
- **The King of the North**
    - In Daniel 11:40 the king of the North "will storm against" the king of the South; the king of the North is the Antichrist himself
    - Hippolytus, Lactantius and Theodoret in the early church, and Archer, Young and Miller in modern times, hold this view, and it places the Antichrist in the Syria and Mesopotamia region
- **The Sudden End**
    - His reign ends abruptly
    - He will "meet His match" and no one will be able to help him when he comes to an end

### Historical Previews: Antiochus Epiphanes

- The extreme nature of these descriptions raises the question of whether such a person could realistically exist
- History provides a "preview" of this type of character in the figure of Antiochus (c. 168 BC)
    - He was the King of Syria
    - He took the title "Antiochus Epiphanes," meaning "the great one"

### The Character of Antiochus Epiphanes

- **The "Maniac" and King of Syria**
    - His name, Epiphanes, can also be interpreted to mean "the maniac"
    - His primary objectives were to blaspheme God and eliminate Judaism from the world
- **Actions Against Judaism**
    - Invaded Jerusalem, killing thousands and selling survivors into slavery
    - Made circumcision a crime punishable by death
    - Made owning a copy of the Law a crime
- **Desecration of the Temple**
    - Erected an altar to Zeus within the Temple
    - Offered a pig on the altar as a direct act of blasphemy
    - Converted the Temple chambers—previously used by priests, Levites, and worshippers—into brothels for prostitutes
- **Outcome of His Reign**
    - He acted as a deliberate, cold-blooded enemy of God attempting to wipe out the Jewish religion and its people
    - He ultimately failed; God used the Maccabee family to lead a revolt that stripped him of power and spared Israel

### The Day of the Lord

- **Definition**
    - A technical term referring to a specific final period of God's ultimate judgment and divine wrath
- **Biblical Frequency**
    - Old Testament: appears 19 times
    - New Testament: appears 4 times
- **Old Testament Descriptions of the Day of the Lord**
    - **Isaiah 13:9**: A day that comes "cruel with both wrath and fierce anger to lay the land desolate"
    - **Jeremiah 46:10**: A day of vengeance where God avenges Himself on His adversaries
    - **Joel 1:15**: Described as destruction coming from the Almighty
    - **Joel 2:11**: Characterized as "great and very terrible"
    - **Joel 2:31**: A day marked by cosmic signs, such as the sun turning into darkness and the moon into blood
    - **Amos 5:20**: A day of darkness

### Further Old Testament Descriptions of the Day of the Lord

- **Zephaniah 1:14**: A day that is "great and dreadful"
- **Zephaniah 1:15**: A day characterized by:
    - Wrath, trouble, and distress
    - Devastation and desolation
    - Darkness, gloom, clouds, and thick darkness

### The Nature of the Day of the Lord

- **Commonly used synonyms and descriptors**
    - **Day of Doom**: Referred to six times
    - **Day of Vengeance**: Referred to four times
    - **The Great Day of His Wrath**: As called in Revelation 6:17
- **Core Significance**
    - It represents the ultimate, severest, and most cataclysmic judgments of God upon the wicked
    - It is the climactic culmination of God's fury and wrath
    - The New Testament describes it as "His day" and the "great day of God Almighty"

### New Testament Descriptions of the Day of the Lord

- **Characterized by fire and intense heat**
    - **Jesus' Parables (Matthew 13)**: Described as a time of burning fire
    - **Jesus' teachings on the Second Coming (Matthew 24 and 25)**: Described as a time when angels come with "flaming fire"
    - **Peter's description**: A time when the elements melt with fervent heat and humanity is consumed in "eschatological fire"

### Paul's Response to the Confusion

- **Addressing the core issues**
    - Paul begins his response by addressing two specific areas:
        - The coming of our Lord Jesus Christ
        - The gathering together to Him

### Paul's Pastoral Approach in 2 Thessalonians 2:1-2

- **Transition to doctrinal core**
    - The word "now" (Greek: *ge*) marks a transition from the prayer in chapter 1 to the central doctrinal matter of the letter: the second coming
- **Gentle and humble tone**
    - Paul addresses the believers as "brethren," emphasizing their equality in Christ
    - He uses a tender approach rather than being pontifical or authoritative
    - This gentleness is intended to deal kindly with a congregation that is deeply perturbed and upset
- **Pastoral motivation**
    - Paul is not attempting to gratify "unlawful curiosity" about the end times
    - His instruction is limited to what is necessary to:
        - Correct their theological errors
        - Restore the comfort they should find in the hope of the Rapture

### The Coming and the Gathering

- **A single, unified event**
    - The Greek construction of "the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ and our gathering together to Him" indicates these are not two separate events
    - A single definite article ties them together, meaning the gathering is a specific aspect of the coming
    - It can be understood as: "the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, specifically our gathering together to Him"
- **Distinguishing the Rapture from other "comings"**
    - The term *parousia* (coming) encompasses many different epochs and events in Christ's return:
        - Coming for His church
        - Coming with His church
        - Coming to judge
        - Coming to set up His kingdom
        - Coming to rule in His kingdom
        - Coming to establish the new heaven and the new earth
    - Paul narrows the focus to the specific event where the Lord comes and believers are gathered to meet Him in the air, which is a description of the Rapture

### The Rapture as the Focus of Confusion

- The Thessalonians' primary concern was understanding how they fit into the timeline of the Day of the Lord
    - Based on their understanding, they felt they shouldn't be included in that period of judgment
- **[Tone of the text]** The use of the personal pronoun "our" in "our Lord" suggests a friendly and hopeful aspect of the event
    - If Paul were focusing solely on the aspect of judgment, he likely would have used "the Lord" instead of "our Lord"

### Greek Terminology for the Gathering

- **Coming**: *parousia*
    - This is the general term for Christ's coming, which Paul relates specifically to the gathering of believers
- **Gathering together**: *epi sunago ges*
    - *Sunago* is the root for "synagogue," meaning a gathering place
    - *Epi* acts as an intensifier, referring to the specific place of our collecting together
    - Some commentators refer to this event as "the muster of the saints"

### The Gathering Together (*epi-sunago-ges*)

- **Biblical usage and meaning**
    - The term *epi-sunago-ges* is rare, appearing in only one other New Testament location: Hebrews 10:25 ("forsake not the assembling together")
    - It carries the core idea of assembling or collecting together
- **Connection to Christ's promises**
    - The gathering is the fulfillment of Jesus' words in John 14:

        > "I'm going to go away. And if I go, I go to prepare a place for you. And I will come again to receive you unto myself, that where I am, there you may be also."

- **The focus of the Thessalonians' concern**
    - Their anxiety was not about the judgment of the wicked, but about the security of the saved
    - They were worried about whether they would be part of this specific "gathering together" to the Lord

### The Source of the Thessalonians' Anxiety

- **[Expectation of Deliverance]** The Thessalonians were not expecting God's fury or eternal wrath
    - According to 1 Thessalonians 1:10, they were waiting for Jesus, who delivers believers from the wrath to come
    - They were looking forward to being gathered into a great congregation to meet Him and go to the place He prepared
- **[Promises of Rest and Glory]** Their focus was on the positive outcomes promised in Paul's earlier teachings:
    - 2 Thessalonians 1:7: promised rest or relief
    - 2 Thessalonians 1:10: promised glory
    - 1 Thessalonians 5:9: Paul reminded them that God is not destined for wrath
- **[The Misconception]** The core of their confusion was the fear that they had already missed the Rapture
    - They likely believed they were currently experiencing the Day of the Lord
    - This led to the terrifying thought that God might have forgotten them

### The Impact of False Teaching

- **[Emotional State]** The Thessalonians were in a state of total distress
    - They were "shaken" and lost their composure
    - They were "severely disturbed" by the news
- **[Sources of Deception]** The false information was delivered through three specific channels:
    - A spirit (supernatural influence)
    - A message (verbal communication)
    - A letter (written communication)
    - These were purportedly from Paul, Silas, or Timothy, claiming the Day of the Lord had arrived
- **[The Core Belief]** The confusion was not about the Day of the Lord *approaching*, but that it had already *arrived*
    - The Greek grammar in verse 2 indicates they believed the event was present
    - This led to existential questions: "What are we doing here?", "What happened to the Rapture?", and "Did God forget us?"
- **[Contributing Factors]** Their vulnerability to this deception was likely heightened by the intense persecution they were enduring

### The Three Avenues of Deception

- The false news was delivered through three distinct means, indicated by the triple use of the Greek word *dia* ("by" or "through")
    - **By a spirit**: This implies a prophetic utterance or direct divine revelation from the Holy Spirit
    - **By a message**: Referred to as *logos*, this represents a spoken word, such as a sermon or verbal speech
    - **By a letter**: This refers to a written communication
- **[The Deception of Authority]** The messages were falsely attributed to Paul, Silas, or Timothy
    - The phrase "as if from us" indicates that the content was being presented as though it originated from the apostles
    - This was particularly effective because only a few months had passed since Paul's previous correspondence, and the Thessalonians held a great love and respect for him

### The Use of Counterfeit Apostolic Authority

- **[The Strategy of Deception]** To make the false claim that the Day of the Lord had arrived convincing, the deceivers had to establish apostolic credentials
    - They didn't just deliver a message; they claimed it possessed the weight of the apostles
- **[Methods of Forgery]** The deception utilized all three previously mentioned avenues to create a sense of absolute certainty:
    - **Spiritual authority**: Claiming the news was a direct revelation to Paul
    - **Verbal authority**: Claiming Paul had preached this specific message
    - **Written authority**: Presenting a forged letter purportedly written by the apostles
- **[Historical Context]** Counterfeit apostolic documents were a significant issue very early in the life of the church
    - Satan utilized forgery from the very outset to spread confusion
    - The early church was forced to develop the ability to distinguish between authentic apostolic teaching and spurious, counterfeit documents
- **[Paul's Stance]** Paul was notably protective of his authorship and did not appreciate letters being falsely attributed to him

### Authentication of Apostolic Letters

- **[Paul's Personal Signature]** Paul used a unique method to distinguish his authentic letters from forgeries
    - In 2 Thessalonians 3:17, he states: "I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand"
    - This served as a distinguishing mark to indicate that the letter was truly his and could not be easily duplicated

### The Psychological Impact of False News

- The false reports that believers would have to endure the Day of the Lord caused extreme distress
- **[The State of the Thessalonians]** According to 2 Thessalonians 2:2, the believers were:
    - **Quickly shaken**: The Greek term implies being hastily or rationally unsettled
    - **Disturbed**: They were deeply shaken from their composure
    - **[The Meaning of 'Shaken']** In the original Greek, the word for composure refers to the *mind*, and to be "shaken" means to be "broken loose" from that mental stability
- **[The Meaning of 'Shaken']** To be "shaken" from one's composure (the mind) is described with violent, graphic imagery:
    - Like a ship being tossed by stormy waves
    - Like a building disintegrating during an earthquake
    - Like a ship breaking loose from its moorings and being tossed by the surf
    - In practical terms, it means losing mental balance, running amok, and operating entirely on emotion, anxiety, and fear
- **[The Meaning of 'Disturbed']** The second verb, "disturbed," implies being alarmed or frightened by fear
    - It carries connotations of clamor, tumult, and crying aloud
- **[Inference of their Belief]** The fact that the believers were "disturbed" and filled with fear proves they believed they would be present during the Day of the Lord
    - If they had believed the Rapture occurred *after* the Day of the Lord, they would have felt excitement or anticipation rather than terror

### The Emotional State of the Church

- The false news resulted in a state of nervous excitement that escalated into full-scale panic
    - The church was experiencing what could be described as a "continuous panic attack"
    - This state caused them to defy a fundamental principle of Christian living: being controlled by truth rather than by emotion
- **[The Nature of their Error]** Their anxiety stemmed from a specific theological misconception
    - They believed they would go through the Day of the Lord and then be taken to glory
    - They expected the sequence to be: Day of the Lord $\rightarrow$ Rapture/Gathering
    - In reality, the expected sequence is: Rapture/Gathering $\rightarrow$ Day of the Lord (for the ungodly)

### The Antichrist as a Corrective Focal Point

- Paul uses the Antichrist as the central focus of his correction in this chapter
- **[Why the Antichrist?]** Because the Antichrist is the specific entity who triggers the events that bring about the Day of the Lord
    - By understanding the role of the Antichrist, believers can understand why they cannot be present during the Day of the Lord

### The Antichrist as a Theological Anchor

- Paul uses the Antichrist as a focal point because the figure is an "already revealed individual"
    - This allows Paul to build a logical case based on established prophecy from Daniel and the teachings of Jesus
- **[The Sequence of Events]** The Day of the Lord cannot occur until specific prerequisite events are triggered
    - According to 2 Thessalonians 2:3, the following must happen first:
        - The apostasy must come
        - The man of lawlessness must be revealed
- **[Reason to Avoid Fear]** Because believers are promised they will have no part in these triggering events, there is no logical basis for living in fear of the Day of the Lord
- **[The Hope of Salvation]** Despite the terror of the upcoming judgment, there are specific biblical promises of redemption:
    - Israel will be saved before the Day of the Lord
    - A great multitude of people from every tongue, tribe, and nation will be saved and sing glory to the Lamb before the day breaks
- **[The Purpose of the Gathering]** The gathering of believers to Jesus is meant to be a source of comfort
    - Rather than being frightened by the Day of the Lord, believers should find peace in their upcoming reunion with Christ
